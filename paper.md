Respiratory viruses can circulate year-round with some showing strong
seasonal patterns. Consistent and reliable forecasts are essential for
public health to provide information for healthcare planning,
interventions such as vaccination, and public communication about
population level risks. ViroReportR is an R package that automates
short-term forecasts and end-to-end routine report generation for common
respiratory viruses (e.g. Sars-CoV-2, Influenza A and Respiratory
Syncytial Virus) from routine surveillance data. Its implementation lies
on using a renewal equation approach to estimate the instantaneous
time-varying reproduction number (R_t) via the EpiEstim package
\[@coriNewFrameworkSoftware2013\], and provides projections at specific
time horizons defined by the user with validation metrics in a
standardized HTML format. The tool is formulated for operational use by
surveillance teams and public health officials, whose needs comprise
robust, transparent, and reproducible results with minimal
configuration. The package is available on CRAN and Github with
documentation, examples, and vignettes.

# Statement of need

Respiratory viruses cause on-going burden across health systems,
especially during respiratory season where their circulation within a
population is more prevalent. Short-term forecasts of cases related to
respiratory infection are key epidemiological indicators to support
public health decision-makers. Routine and reproducible multi-pathogen
forecast reports should contain standardized uncertainty, model
validation, and a reproducible workflow to aide the public health
community, researchers, and ensure public trust. ViroReportR addresses
this issue directly by providing an automated reporting workflow, where
users supply daily counts in a three-column dataset (`date`,
`disease_type`, `confirm`), optionally specifying serial interval
parameters, forecast horizon and seasonal patterns, and obtain a HTML
report with forecasts plots, uncertainty intervals, (R_t) estimates, and
validation metrics across pathogens. ViroReportR focuses on operational
transparency and replicability, making it a well-suited and reliable
tool to public health surveillance teams and policy makers who need
daily to weekly briefs generated by routine data.

# State of the field

There are a variety of packages that provides tools for surveillance,
epidemic parameter estimation, nowcasting, or time-series forecasting.
However, they usually target a different user group or different layers
of the analytic stack compared to ViroReportR. None provide a fully
automated multi-pathogen reporting tool for routine public health
surveillance. Below, we briefly summarize the most relevant packages and
provide clarity on how ViroReportR is distinctively situated and filling
a specific gap.

1.  `EpiNow` and `EpiNow2`: both of these packages estimate time-varying
    reproduction number, growth rate, and doubling time using a range of
    open-source tools. `EpiNow` is now deprecated in favor of `EpiNow2`.
    The aim of these packages is to provide a framework that is informed
    by community feedback and actively supported, avoiding potential
    limitations of naive implementations. Forecasting is also available,
    by extending the approach used for estimation. However, outputs are
    provided in the form of R objects or plots and not as a combined
    report suitable for public health. Due to the Bayesian modelling
    framework utilized in these packages, computational time may be a
    constraint for users especially for longer time-series.
2.  `surveillance`: this package focuses on statistical methods for
    monitoring and modelling of time series of counts, proportion and
    categorical data, common in epidemiological settings. With a
    specific focus on aberration detection in count time series of
    communicable diseases, outbreak detection, and endemic-epidemic
    modelling framework, including visualization and simulation tools.
    Similar to `EpiNow` and `EpiNow2` this package doesn’t offer
    short-term forecasts reporting tools.
3.  `Sitrep`: provides report templates for epidemiological surveys and
    outbreaks reports. The templates are intended for *Medecins Sans
    Frontieres* analyses, however, have a generic format that can be
    used by general epidemiological community. Their reports focuses on
    epidemiological indicators and standardize common epidemiological
    analyses, such as attack rates and descriptive analyses of outbreak
    data. It does not provide short-term forecasts of daily counts
    across pathogens.
4.  `epi_predict`: offers a framework for building transformation and
    forecasting pipelines tailored for epidemiological time-series data.
    It uses a variety of forecasting techniques and also provides back
    testing tools for validation. However, it does not offer a tool for
    automated report generation, requiring the user to assemble their
    own reporting pipeline on top of `epi_predict`.
5.  `PROF`: package designed for respiratory disease forecasting. `PROF`
    supports modelling of influenza and COVID-19 using either
    compartmental modelling or a baseline statistical model. However, it
    doesn’t generalize for arbitrary user-defined pathogens and does not
    include a unified automated reporting structure for surveillance
    purposes.

Across `CRAN` there are a variety of packages providing strong modelling
capabilities, reporting templates, forecasting and outbreak detection.
However, none of them integrates multiple component of the
epidemiological analysis stack into a single automated workflow.
`ViroReporR`: 1. accept multi-pathogen surveillance data in a format of
daily counts with columns `date`, `confirm`, and `disease_type`. 2.
produces (R_t)-based forecasts with uncertainty quantification. 3.
performs backtesting for validation and summarizes standard forecasting
metrics. 4. renders a standardized HTML report for use on weekly briefs.

`ViroReportR` stands between low-level modelling libraries, such as
`EpiNow2`, and `EpiEstim`, and reporting templates libraries such as
`Sitrep`, by integrating modelling, forecasting and reporting into a
single tool. Filling a unique niche, `ViroReportR` offers an
operationally tailored multi-pathogen reporting tool specifically for
virus respiratory pathogens.

# Software design

Our key design goals were to provide a flexible and easy to use report
generation tool, while keeping the overall structure of the report
fixed. The `ViroReportR` package works around a main function
[`generate_forecast_report()`](https://bccdc-phsa.github.io/ViroReportR/reference/generate_forecast_report.md)
that calls all other functions for data processing, fitting,
forecasting, validation, and renders a final markdown file based on the
template provided in the package.

We designed the workflow to be as simple as possible, only needing a
cleaned data file of the daily counts of a given set of respiratory
viruses. The number of pathogen types used to generate the report are
inferred directly from the structure of the data. The required format of
the data is 3 columns with `date`, `disease_type` and `confirm`
(confirmed cases), which is in keeping with the design in `EpiNow2`. If
the user has added any additional viruses other than Influenza A,
SARS-CoV2 and RSV, they will also need to add serial interval
distribution parameters for those viruses. The overall text and
explanations on the report are not dependent on results. Adding new
virus provides more tabs, plots, and summary tables on the final html
report. In addition, some respiratory viruses follow a clear respiratory
season, whereas others may not. Thus, we include an option for users to
provide seasonal period for each virus as needed. Currently, we provide
estimations using the `EpiEstim` packages
\[@coriNewFrameworkSoftware2013\], but we have designed `ViroReportR` to
be model agnostic and allow for inclusion of other model estimation
methods in future releases.

# Research impact statement

The `ViroReportR` package was developed primarily to streamline
forecasting and reporting at the BC Centre for Disease Control. As such,
it has been deployed for monitoring by the Public Health Response team
during the 2025/26 respiratory season and hosted on the Centre’s Posit
Connect infrastrcture. It has also been deployed to monitor and forecast
the number of COVID-19, Influenza A, and Respiratory Syncytial Virus at
mass gathering events within Vancouver, British Columbia during the 2026
FIFA world cup.
